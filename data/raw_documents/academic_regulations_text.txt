CHAPTER 1: PREAMBLE, PROGRAM NOMENCLATURE & DEGREE
DURATIONS
Clause 1.1: B.Tech Degree Normal Duration and Credit Requirement
The Bachelor of Technology (B.Tech) program shall be a four-year full-time undergraduate program comprising eight
(8) regular semesters. Every candidate enrolled in the B.Tech program must successfully complete a minimum of one
hundred and sixty (160) academic credits as prescribed by the respective Board of Studies. A student shall be
deemed eligible for the award of the B.Tech degree only upon clearing all mandatory theory courses, laboratory
sessions, internships, and capstone projects.
Clause 1.2: Maximum Duration for Program Completion
The maximum allowable duration for completing all degree requirements for the 4-year B.Tech program shall be
normal duration plus two (2) years, which corresponds to six (6) academic years or twelve (12) consecutive
semesters from the date of initial registration. If a student fails to fulfill all academic and credit requirements within this
twelve-semester ceiling, their registration shall stand automatically cancelled without further notification.
Clause 1.3: Lateral Entry Students Scheme
Students admitted directly into the third semester under the Lateral Entry scheme (diploma holders or B.Sc
graduates) shall undergo a six-semester program (Semesters 3 through 8). Lateral entry candidates must earn a
minimum of one hundred and twenty (120) academic credits. The maximum permissible time for lateral entry
candidates to complete degree requirements is five (5) academic years (ten semesters).
Clause 1.4: Medium of Instruction and Examination
The official medium of instruction, course delivery, assessments, laboratory assignments, and End Semester
Assessments (ESA) shall be strictly English across all academic departments. All project reports, dissertations, and
oral defenses must be delivered exclusively in the English language.

CHAPTER 2: COURSE REGISTRATION, CREDIT STRUCTURE & ADD/DROP
POLICY
Clause 2.1: Credit Definition and Calculation System
Academic credits are computed on a weekly basis per semester: One (1) hour of lecture (L) per week equals 1 credit;
one (1) hour of tutorial (T) per week equals 1 credit; two (2) hours of laboratory or practical session (P) per week
equals 1 credit. Non-credit mandatory audit courses are designated with zero credits on the grade sheet.
Clause 2.2: Permissible Semester Credit Load Limits
A student is required to register for a minimum of 16 credits and a normal maximum of 24 credits in any given regular
semester. Under special academic circumstances, students with a cumulative grade point average (CGPA) of 8.50 or
above may register for up to 28 credits in a semester, subject to the explicit written approval of the Department
Faculty Advisor and the Dean of Academic Affairs.
Clause 2.3: Course Add/Drop Period and Deadlines
Students are permitted to modify their course registrations by adding or dropping courses strictly within the first ten
(10) instructional days of the commencement of the semester. No request for addition of courses shall be entertained
after the tenth instructional day under any circumstances.
Clause 2.4: Course Withdrawal Policy
A student may formally withdraw from a registered elective course up to the end of the eighth (8th) instructional week
of the semester. A course withdrawn under this provision shall appear on the grade card with the grade letter 'W'. The
grade 'W' carries zero grade points and does not impact SGPA or CGPA. Withdrawn courses carry no fee refunds
and cannot bring the total active credit load below 16 credits.
Clause 2.5: Audit Course Registration Guidelines
Students may register for at most one (1) audit course per semester. Audit courses require at least 75% attendance.
Students satisfying attendance shall receive the grade 'AU' on their grade card. Audit courses carry zero grade points
and do not count toward cumulative grade calculations.

CHAPTER 3: ATTENDANCE POLIISAS, CONDONATION & DETENTION (NSAR)
Clause 3.1: Mandatory 85% Attendance Threshold
Every student is required to maintain a minimum attendance of eighty-five percent (85%) of the total instructional
hours delivered in each individual registered theory course and practical course in order to become eligible to appear
for the End Semester Assessment (ESA) of that course.
Clause 3.2: Condonation of Attendance Shortage on Valid Grounds
Condonation of attendance shortage up to a maximum of ten percent (10%)—allowing eligibility for students with
attendance between 75.0% and 84.9%—may be granted strictly by the Vice-Chancellor on the recommendation of
the Attendance Review Committee. Condonation is permitted solely for valid medical hospitalization or official
university deputation (e.g., inter-university sports or academic competitions). The student must submit official medical
proof within three (3) working days of returning to campus and pay a non-refundable Condonation Fee of INR 2,000
per course.
Clause 3.3: Detention under NSAR (Not Satisfied Attendance Requirement)
Any student whose final attendance in a course falls below 75.0% under any circumstance (including medical illness)
shall NOT be eligible for condonation and shall be declared detained in that course under grade code 'NSAR'. The
student shall be prohibited from writing the ESA for that course and must mandatorily re-register for the entire course
during the summer fast-track semester or subsequent regular semester.
Clause 3.4: Practical Laboratory Attendance Rules
One hundred percent (100%) completion of prescribed laboratory experiments and practical write-ups is compulsory.
Any student missing a lab session must attend a scheduled compensatory lab session before the end of the
semester. Shortage below 85% in laboratory courses is strictly non-condonable.

CHAPTER 4: EVALUATION SCHEME & CONTINUOUS INTERNAL EVALUATION
(ISA)
Clause 4.1: Overall Course Weightage Distribution
The performance of a student in each academic course is assessed through two main evaluation mechanisms:
Internal Semester Assessment (ISA) carrying 50% of total course marks, and End Semester Assessment (ESA)
carrying 50% of total course marks.
Clause 4.2: Components and Breakdown of ISA (50 Marks)
The 50 marks allocated to Internal Semester Assessment (ISA) are partitioned as follows:
  Internal Assessment Tests (IAT): Two centralized written tests of 25 marks each conducted across the semester. The
aggregate is scaled to 30 marks.
 Quizzes & Surprise Assessments: Two online or in-class objective quizzes of 5 marks each, contributing a total of 10
marks.
 Experiential Learning & Mini-Project Assignment: Open-ended problem-solving assignment or course seminar
evaluated for 10 marks.
Clause 4.3: Minimum ISA Threshold for Eligibility to Write ESA
To be eligible to appear in the End Semester Assessment (ESA) of any course, a student must secure a minimum of
forty percent (40%) marks in the Internal Semester Assessment (ISA), which corresponds to at least twenty (20)
marks out of fifty (50). Students obtaining less than 20 marks in ISA are categorized as 'NE' (Not Eligible) and shall
NOT be permitted to write the ESA.
Clause 4.4: Laboratory and Practical Course Evaluation
Laboratory courses are evaluated for 100 marks: 60 marks for continuous weekly observation, journal submission,
and viva-voce; 40 marks for the comprehensive final lab practical examination. A minimum score of 50% in aggregate
is required to clear laboratory subjects.

CHAPTER 5: END SEMESTER ASSESSMENT (ESA) & GRADING SYSTEM
Clause 5.1: ESA Blueprint and Duration
The End Semester Assessment (ESA) for theory courses is a three-hour closed-book centralized written examination
set for 100 marks and scaled down to 50 marks. Question papers contain five distinct modules with internal choice
within each module.
Clause 5.2: Minimum Passing Standards in a Course
A candidate shall be declared to have passed a course if and only if they obtain: (i) at least 40% marks in the End
Semester Assessment (minimum 20 out of 50 scaled marks, or 40 out of 100 raw marks), AND (ii) an aggregate of at
least 40% marks combined across ISA and ESA (minimum 40 marks out of 100).

Clause 5.4: SGPA and CGPA Mathematical Formulations
The Semester Grade Point Average (SGPA) is computed as: SGPA = Sum(C_i * G_i) / Sum(C_i), where C_i
represents course credits and G_i represents grade points earned. The Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) is
computed across all registered courses from the first semester up to the current semester in identical fashion.

CHAPTER 6: ACADEMIC PROGRESSION, DETAINMENT & MINIMUM CREDITS
Clause 6.1: Promotion Criteria to 3rd Semester (Second Year)
To be eligible for promotion from the first year to the third semester (commencement of 2nd year), a student must
have earned at least 60% of the total cumulative credits prescribed for the first and second semesters combined (i.e.,
at least 24 credits out of 40 first-year credits).
Clause 6.2: Promotion Criteria to 5th Semester (Third Year) - Zero Backlog Rule
To be promoted to the fifth semester (commencement of 3rd year), a student must fulfill two concurrent criteria: (i)
Must have successfully cleared ALL courses of the first year (1st and 2nd semesters) with zero pending backlogs,
AND (ii) Must have earned at least 60% of the total credits prescribed for the second year (3rd and 4th semesters).
Clause 6.3: Promotion Criteria to 7th Semester (Fourth Year)
To register for the seventh semester (final year), a candidate must: (i) have passed ALL courses of the second year
(3rd and 4th semesters) with zero pending backlogs, AND (ii) have earned at least 60% of the total credits prescribed
for the third year (5th and 6th semesters).
Clause 6.4: Year Detention Policy
Any student failing to satisfy the credit promotion or zero-backlog prerequisite rules specified in Clauses 6.1 through
6.3 shall be declared Year-Detained. A detained student cannot attend higher semester classes and must spend an
academic year clearing backlog courses before progressing.
Clause 6.5: Minimum CGPA Requirement for Degree Award
A student must secure a cumulative CGPA of not less than 5.00 at the conclusion of the eighth semester to be
eligible for the award of B.Tech Degree. Candidates securing a CGPA between 4.00 and 4.99 must register for grade
improvement in the summer semester to attain the 5.00 threshold.

CHAPTER 7: EXAMINATION MALPRACTICE, CODE OF CONDUCT &
PENALTIES
Clause 7.1: Classification of Examination Malpractice Offences
Academic dishonesty during any quiz, ISA, or ESA is subject to strict penalties. Prohibited acts include: carrying
written notes/chits, electronic gadgets, programmable calculators, smart watches, mobile phones into exam halls,
copying, exchange of answer booklets, and impersonation.
Clause 7.2: Category I Penalty - Unauthorized Possession Without Copying
Possession of unauthorized study materials, manuscripts, formula sheets, or mobile devices in turned-off state
without evidence of copying: Penalty entails immediate confiscation of material, cancellation of the examination in
that specific course, and award of 'F' grade for that course.
Clause 7.3: Category II Penalty - Active Copying or Digital Transmission
Active copying from unauthorized material, receiving or transmitting answers via smartphone/smartwatch, or assisting
other examinees: Penalty entails cancellation of all examinations registered in the current semester, award of 'F'
grade in all current courses, and debarment for one (1) subsequent regular semester.
Clause 7.4: Category III Penalty - Impersonation and Severe Misconduct
Impersonation of an examinee, assault or threat to invigilators, or intentional destruction of answer scripts: Immediate
expulsion of the candidate from the university for two (2) academic years, cancellation of past semester results, and
lodging of a formal police First Information Report (FIR).
Clause 7.5: Examination Disciplinary Committee (EDC) Jurisdiction
All reported malpractice cases are adjudicated by the five-member Examination Disciplinary Committee (EDC)
chaired by the Dean of Student Affairs. The student is afforded an opportunity for a personal hearing within seven (7)
working days of the incident before the final penalty is ratified.

CHAPTER 8: RE-EVALUATION, SCRIPT INSPECTION & CHALLENGE
VALUATION
Clause 8.1: Photostat Copy of Answer Booklet Inspection
A student desiring to review their evaluated ESA answer script may apply for a certified photocopy within five (5)
working days from the date of online publication of results. The application fee is INR 500 per course
(non-refundable). Scripts are made available digitally within 3 working days.
Clause 8.2: Re-evaluation Procedure and Deadlines
Students may apply for re-evaluation of theory answer scripts within ten (10) working days of result declaration upon
payment of a re-evaluation fee of INR 1,200 per course. Re-evaluation is conducted through a double-blind
independent assessment by a subject expert outside the teaching faculty.
Clause 8.3: Mark Revision and Fee Refund Rules
If the marks obtained in re-evaluation exceed the original marks by five percent (5%) or more of the maximum
examination marks, the re-evaluated marks shall replace the original marks. If marks increase by fifteen percent
(15%) or more, the script is sent to a third independent evaluator whose score is final, and the entire re-evaluation fee
of INR 1,200 shall be refunded to the student. If the change is less than 5%, the original marks remain unaltered.
Clause 8.4: Challenge Valuation Scheme
If a student remains aggrieved following re-evaluation, they may apply for Challenge Valuation within seven (7) days
of re-evaluation results by depositing INR 5,000. Evaluation is conducted jointly by a panel of two senior professors in
the presence of the Controller of Examinations.

CHAPTER 9: MAKE-UP EXAMS, SUMMER FAST-TRACK & REMEDIAL SCHEME
Clause 9.1: Eligibility Criteria for Make-up Examinations
Make-up examinations are NOT supplementary or backlog exams. Make-up exams are permissible strictly for
students who were absent from the regular ESA due to documented medical emergency hospitalization or official
national representation, and who possess at least 60% in ISA. Students with 'F', 'NE', or 'NSAR' grades are
completely barred from make-up exams.
Clause 9.2: Timing and Grade Capping in Make-up Exams
Make-up examinations are conducted within two (2) weeks following the announcement of regular ESA results. The
maximum letter grade awarded in a make-up examination shall be capped at grade 'A' (Grade Point 8), irrespective of
whether the numerical score falls in the 'S' or 'O' range.
Clause 9.3: Summer Fast-Track Semester Structure
A special summer fast-track semester of six (6) to eight (8) weeks duration is conducted during June and July. A
student may register for a maximum of two (2) theory courses and one (1) laboratory course (totaling not more than
ten [10] credits) for re-registration or backlog clearance.
Clause 9.4: Remedial Support System
Departments conduct mandatory remedial tutorial sessions on Saturdays for students scoring below 50% in ISA.
Remedial attendance is tracked by the Academic Mentoring Cell and is a prerequisite for receiving borderline
condonation.

CHAPTER 10: HONOURS, MINORS, CURRICULAR REVISIONS & INTERNSHIPS
Clause 10.1: B.Tech with Honours Degree Requirements
An undergraduate student can earn a B.Tech with Honours by meeting the following strict criteria: (i) Must possess a
cumulative CGPA of 8.50 or higher at the end of the fourth semester with zero backlogs; (ii) Must register for and
successfully complete an additional eighteen to twenty (18-20) advanced departmental credits across semesters 5
through 8; (iii) Must maintain a CGPA of >= 8.50 until graduation without any grade 'F' throughout the degree.
Clause 10.2: B.Tech with Minor Degree Requirements
To obtain a Minor in an interdisciplinary engineering discipline, a student with a minimum CGPA of 7.00 may earn
eighteen (18) additional credits offered by the secondary department. Minor courses must be completed alongside
regular coursework within the prescribed eight semesters.
Clause 10.3: Mandatory Industrial Internship Guidelines
Every student must undergo a mandatory full-time industrial internship of a minimum duration of six (6) weeks during
the summer vacation between the sixth (6th) and seventh (7th) semesters. The internship carries three (3) academic
credits, evaluated in the seventh semester through an industrial mentor evaluation, written technical report, and an
oral viva-voce defense.
Clause 10.4: Final Year Capstone Project Structure
The eighth semester is dedicated to the Capstone Major Project carrying twelve (12) credits. Projects are evaluated
by an external and internal committee in three phases: Phase I (Problem Formulation & Literature Review - 20%),
Phase II (Mid-term Prototype - 30%), and Final Defense & Demonstration (50%).
